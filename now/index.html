How this is computed

Each planet’s position comes from JPL’s long-range Keplerian elements (the Standish tables, fitted for 3000 BC – 3000 AD), solved in your browser at the instant shown. That is the same data and the same solver as the 3D orrery, so the two always agree. Positions are geometric: light-travel time and aberration are not applied. Checked against the precise VSOP87 theory behind the night sky page, they are within about 10 arcminutes (a third of the Moon’s width) from 2016 to 2036, the inner planets within 5. Times are UTC.

These are astronomical positions, not a horoscope: no signs, no houses, no predictions. For a professional-grade ephemeris use JPL Horizons.

Questions

Which planets can I see tonight?

The summary at the top names them for the current moment. Planets listed under the evening sky are east of the Sun, so they are in the sky after sunset, in the general direction the Sun went down, though one close to the Sun may set before the sky is dark; those under the morning sky rise before the Sun. A planet more than 150° from the Sun is near opposition and up most of the night. At opposition itself the Sun, Earth and the planet line up in a syzygy, and the planet is at about its closest and brightest for the year. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn are naked-eye objects; Uranus is at the limit of naked-eye visibility from a dark site and easy in binoculars, and Neptune needs a telescope. For what is above your own horizon tonight, and when, use the night sky page.

What does “retrograde” mean in the table?

Apparent retrograde motion: for a few weeks or months around opposition a planet appears to drift backwards (westward) against the stars, because Earth is overtaking it on the inside — or, for Mercury and Venus, because they are overtaking Earth. The planet never actually reverses; it is a line-of-sight effect. The table checks each planet’s geocentric longitude a day either side of now. It has nothing to do with astrology.

How accurate are these positions?

Within about 10 arcminutes (a third of the Moon’s width) of a precise ephemeris from 2016 to 2036, the inner planets within 5, drifting to a fraction of a degree at the ends of the 3000 BC – 3000 AD range. Distances are centre to centre. This page is an educational tool, not a navigational or scientific reference.

Can I see where the planets were, or will be, on another date?

Yes — the 3D orrery takes any date and time from 3000 BC to 3000 AD, plays time forwards or backwards, follows any planet, and jumps straight to famous alignments from its events menu. Far back in time the dates are in the proleptic Gregorian calendar and the time of day is only approximate, out by hours in antiquity, because Earth’s spin has slowed by an amount known only roughly.
